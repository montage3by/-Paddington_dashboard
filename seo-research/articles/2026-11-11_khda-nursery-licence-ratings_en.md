# KHDA and Nurseries in Dubai: How to Check a Licence and What "KHDA Rating" Really Means

If you've been looking at nurseries this month, you've probably seen the news. On 6 November 2026, Dubai Police said they had closed 33 unlicensed nurseries and arrested 34 people connected to them. Some of the nurseries were in residential buildings, and police said the premises didn't meet basic hygiene and safety standards. Their advice to parents was simple: check that a nursery holds a valid licence before you enrol, and don't choose on low fees or advertising alone.

That advice raises two practical questions. How do you check a licence? And what about the KHDA rating that so many parents search for?

In short, every legal nursery in Dubai is licensed by KHDA, but nurseries don't get a published KHDA rating the way schools do. Here's what that means when you're choosing one.

## What is KHDA, and what does it do for nurseries?

The Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA) regulates private education in Dubai: schools, higher education and early childhood centres. "Early childhood centre", or ECC, is the official term for a nursery. To operate legally, a nursery needs KHDA approval in the form of an Educational Services Permit, and it can admit children from 45 days to six years old.

The sector is big and still growing. In the 2024–25 academic year, 29,634 children attended 312 private early childhood centres in Dubai, 8% more children than the year before. KHDA expects another 17 centres to open in 2026–27, adding more than 1,700 places. More choice is good news, but it also means more nurseries to compare.

## Is there a KHDA rating for nurseries?

Not at the moment.

Private schools in Dubai are inspected and graded on a six-point scale from Outstanding to Very weak, and those ratings are public. After a pause, school inspections are returning in 2026–27. This is the "KHDA rating" most parents have in mind.

Standalone nurseries don't receive a comparable grade. What KHDA has done is set out what good early years provision should look like:

- **A quality framework.** KHDA launched the Quality Framework for Early Childhood Care and Education in November 2025. It was formally approved in 2026 through Administrative Resolution No. (13). The framework covers five areas: learning and curriculum, partnership with families, the learning environment, health and safeguarding, and leadership. KHDA has said the framework lays the foundation for a future quality assurance system, but for now it doesn't bring in ratings for nurseries.
- **Health and safety standards.** These were published in January 2026 and cover areas such as infection control, nutrition, incident management and cybersecurity.
- **Compliance inspections.** A compliance guide KHDA issued in September 2026 lists several types of visit: annual visits, visits after a complaint, follow-up visits and joint inspections with other government bodies. During unannounced visits, centres must give inspectors access to records and CCTV recordings.

So licensed nurseries are checked. There just isn't a single grade you can use to compare them.

### What "KHDA approved" means on a nursery's website

It means the nursery is licensed. That's an important minimum, but it isn't a quality grade. If a nursery mentions a KHDA rating, ask what it refers to. It may be the rating of a school the nursery is linked to: early years classes inside schools are assessed as part of the school's own inspection.

## How to check a nursery's licence in four steps

### 1. Ask for the permit number

A licensed nursery will give you its KHDA permit number without any fuss. Many display the licence at reception. If staff are vague or promise to send it "later", treat that as a warning sign.

### 2. Find the nursery in the KHDA directory

KHDA keeps a directory of early childhood centres on its website and in its app. You can search by name or area. Check that the name and location match the place you actually visited. This matters for nurseries with several branches, because each branch has its own entry in the directory.

### 3. Ask how health and first aid are handled

Ask who looks after first aid and medication, and what happens when a child becomes unwell during the day. Many Dubai nurseries employ a nurse licensed by the Dubai Health Authority (DHA). If yours does, ask whether the nurse is on site for the full opening hours.

### 4. Check before you pay anything

Do these checks before you pay a registration fee or deposit. Licensed nurseries are used to these questions, and a good one will be glad you asked.

## Red flags of unlicensed childcare

- **No sign of a licence.** The nursery isn't in the KHDA directory, or it can't give you a permit number.
- **A flat or villa with no signage**, presented as a "small home nursery".
- **Fees that seem too low.** Dubai Police said the nurseries closed in November mainly targeted low-income families with cheap childcare.
- **Cash only, with no contract or receipts.**
- **A "home learning hub" during a normal school term.** In April 2026, KHDA introduced centre-led home-based learning: small groups of up to eight children, or one-to-one care, run by licensed nurseries. These arrangements are allowed only during government-mandated distance learning and only through a licensed centre. Parents can't apply for them directly, and an independent operator can't offer them.

If you suspect a nursery is operating without a licence, you can report it to Dubai Police on **901**, or through the Dubai Police website or app.

## Beyond the licence: questions to ask on a tour

Without a nursery rating to rely on, your visit becomes the main source of information. These questions tend to show the most:

- What is the staff-to-child ratio in my child's age group, and does it change at the start and end of the day?
- What qualifications do the teachers have, and how long have they worked here?
- Who can collect my child, and how is that checked?
- How does the building control who comes in, and where are the cameras?
- What is the sickness policy, and when should my child stay at home?
- Where is the food prepared, and how are allergies managed?
- How much time do children spend outside, and what happens in the hottest months?
- How will you tell me about my child's day?

Visit at least two or three nurseries if you can. Clear, specific answers are a good sign. Vague ones are worth noting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does KHDA rate nurseries in Dubai?

No. KHDA licenses and inspects early childhood centres but doesn't currently publish quality ratings for them. Its Quality Framework for Early Childhood Care and Education, approved in 2026, is the basis for a future quality assurance system.

### How do I know if a nursery in Dubai is licensed?

Ask for the KHDA permit number and look the nursery up in the KHDA early childhood centres directory on the KHDA website or app. Make sure the name and address match the branch you visited.

### Can a nursery operate from a private home in Dubai?

Only in a narrow case. Since April 2026, licensed nurseries can run small home-based learning groups, but only during government-mandated distance learning. If someone offers a "home nursery" during a normal term, check them in the KHDA directory before going any further.

### How do I report an unlicensed nursery?

Call Dubai Police on 901, or use the Dubai Police website or app.

### From what age can children attend nursery in Dubai?

Early childhood centres in Dubai can admit children from 45 days to six years old.
