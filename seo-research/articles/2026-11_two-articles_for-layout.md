# Winter Camp in Dubai for Toddlers and Preschoolers: How to Choose

The UAE winter break runs from 14 December 2026 to 3 January 2027, and classes resume on 4 January. For families with young children, that's three weeks to plan, often while one or both parents are still working. A good winter camp gives little ones a routine, new friends and plenty to do. Here's how to choose one that suits a toddler or preschooler.

## Why a winter camp helps young children

Three weeks without a routine is a long time for a two- or three-year-old. A winter camp keeps the rhythm of the day familiar: arrival, play, food, rest and home time. It also gives children the chance to spend time with others their age, which many toddlers miss during long holidays.

For parents, a holiday camp in Dubai fills the gap between the end of term and the end of annual leave. It also helps when relatives can't fly in to help.

## What makes a good winter camp for under-5s

Most kids winter camp programmes in Dubai are built for school-age children, with sports tournaments, trips and long, busy days. Toddlers and preschoolers need something gentler.

### Groups by age

Look for small groups split by age, so a two-year-old isn't keeping pace with six-year-olds. Activities, snacks and rest should match what children of that age can manage.

### A familiar daily rhythm

Meals, rest time and quiet moments matter as much as activities. Ask how the day is structured from drop-off to pick-up, and where children rest if they still nap.

### Time indoors and outdoors

December is one of the best months for outdoor play in Dubai. A camp with outdoor space and indoor rooms for music, movement and art keeps the day balanced, whatever the weather does.

### Staff who know early years

The adults leading the day matter most. Ask who runs the camp, whether they work with young children all year, and how many adults are with each group.

### Health and safety

Check whether there is a nurse on site, how the camp handles allergies, and who is allowed to collect your child. These details are easy to ask about and hard to fix later.

## Why a nursery winter camp suits little ones

A camp run by a nursery is designed around young children from the start. Staff know early years routines, the rooms are set up for small children, and the day moves at a pace toddlers can enjoy. Toilets, tables, books and toys are all the right size, and there are quiet spaces for children who need a break.

If your child is starting nursery in the new year, a winter camp at the same nursery can also be a gentle first step. Your child gets to know the building and some of the team before the term begins. Read more about settling in in our guide to choosing a British nursery in Dubai.

## Finding a winter camp near me

When you search for a winter camp near me, distance matters more than it seems. During the holidays, traffic in Dubai can change from day to day. A camp ten to fifteen minutes from home or work makes drop-off and pick-up easier for everyone, especially with a sleepy toddler in the back seat.

It also helps to check:

- daily hours, and whether early drop-off or late pick-up is possible;
- whether you can book single weeks or only the full camp;
- what happens on public holidays during the break.

## Questions to ask before booking

- Which age groups does the camp accept?
- How many children are in each group?
- What does a typical day look like?
- Who leads the activities?
- Are meals and snacks included?
- Is there a nurse on site?
- Can my child join for one week, or only for the full camp?
- Can we visit before the camp starts?

## What to pack for winter camp

December mornings in Dubai can be cool, while afternoons are often warm and sunny. Dress your child in layers and label everything.

- A cap or sun hat and children's sunscreen
- A light jacket or jumper for the morning
- A spare set of clothes
- A water bottle
- Comfortable closed shoes for outdoor play
- Snacks or lunch, if the camp doesn't provide food
- Nappies and wipes for younger children
- A small comfort toy for rest time

# EYFS Curriculum Explained: What Your Child Learns from Baby Room to FS2

Many nurseries in Dubai describe themselves as "British" or "EYFS". But what does the EYFS actually mean for your child on an ordinary Tuesday morning? Here's a plain-English guide to the Early Years Foundation Stage: what it is, the seven areas of learning, how progress is checked, and what learning looks like at each age, from the baby room to FS2.

## What is the EYFS?

The EYFS (Early Years Foundation Stage) is the framework used in England for children from birth to five. It sets out how young children learn and develop, and how nurseries and schools should support them. In Dubai, British nurseries and British schools follow the same framework, so a child who starts at a British nursery moves into a British school's Foundation Stage without a change of approach.

You'll often see two school-style names in Dubai:

- **FS1** – for children aged three to four;
- **FS2** – for children aged four to five, the last year of the EYFS before Year 1.

From the 2026–27 academic year, the UAE counts a child's age for FS1 and FS2 on 31 December of the year they start, rather than 31 August. A child needs to be three by 31 December to start FS1, and four to start FS2.

## The seven areas of learning

The EYFS divides early learning into seven areas. Three are called **prime areas**, because they underpin everything else and matter most for the youngest children:

1. **Communication and language** – listening, understanding and speaking.
2. **Personal, social and emotional development** – feelings, friendships, confidence and self-care.
3. **Physical development** – big movements like climbing and running, and fine motor skills like holding a crayon.

The other four are **specific areas**, which grow in importance as children get older:

4. **Literacy** – early reading and writing.
5. **Mathematics** – numbers, shapes, patterns and early problem-solving.
6. **Understanding the world** – people, places, nature and how things work.
7. **Expressive arts and design** – art, music, dance, role-play and imagination.

## How children learn in the EYFS

The EYFS is play-based. It doesn't mean children "just play" – it means play is the main way they learn. The framework describes three ways children learn best:

- **Playing and exploring** – investigating and trying things out.
- **Active learning** – concentrating, keeping going and enjoying achievements.
- **Creating and thinking critically** – having their own ideas and finding ways to do things.

Adults plan the environment and activities around each child's interests and next steps, rather than teaching the whole group the same thing at the same time.

## How progress is checked

The EYFS has two formal checkpoints in England:

- a **progress check at age two**, which looks at the prime areas;
- the **EYFS profile** at the end of the Reception year, which compares each child with 17 **Early Learning Goals**. In British schools in Dubai, the equivalent year is usually FS2.

Between these points, staff observe children during everyday play and share progress with parents.

## EYFS stage by stage

### 0–1 years: the very beginning

For babies, the focus is on feeling safe, loved and connected. Warm, responsive care builds trust. Babies develop early communication through eye contact, sounds and gestures, build core strength by rolling, sitting and crawling, and start to explore the world around them.

### 1–2 years: first steps in social development

Toddlers want to do things themselves: feeding, choosing, trying. Vocabulary grows quickly, and simple sentences appear. Children start playing alongside others and, with support, begin to share and take turns. Walking, climbing and stacking develop coordination.

### 2–3 years: growing confidence

Children manage more personal tasks, like dressing and tidying up, and make simple choices. They form longer sentences, ask questions and follow instructions. Play becomes more imaginative and cooperative, and children learn to understand other people's feelings.

### 3–4 years (FS1): foundations of learning

The specific areas come to the fore. Children begin to recognise letters, try early writing, count, notice shapes and patterns, and explore simple science through nature and experiments. Role-play and group activities build empathy and self-control.

### 4–5 years (FS2): ready for school

Children read simple words, write short sentences and talk confidently. They work with numbers and simple calculations, ask questions about cause and effect, and express themselves through art, music and dance. Teamwork and managing emotions prepare them for Year 1.
