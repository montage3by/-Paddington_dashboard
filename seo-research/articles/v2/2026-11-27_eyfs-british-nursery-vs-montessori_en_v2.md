# How a British Nursery Works: The EYFS and How It Compares with Montessori

Dubai parents can choose between several early years approaches, and nursery websites are full of terms that aren't always explained. If you're looking at a British nursery, the key word is EYFS. Here's what it means in practice, what a day looks like, and how it differs from Montessori, the other approach many families consider.

## What is the EYFS?

The EYFS (Early Years Foundation Stage) is the framework England uses for children from birth to five. It's the base of the British curriculum in the early years, in Dubai and in the UK. Most British nurseries in Dubai follow it, and so do the FS1 and FS2 classes of British schools.

The EYFS sets out what children should learn, how adults should support them and how progress is checked. It covers care as well as learning, from safety and wellbeing to early reading and maths.

## Seven areas of learning

The EYFS groups learning into seven areas.

### Three prime areas

The prime areas matter most in the first years:

- communication and language;
- physical development;
- personal, social and emotional development.

### Four specific areas

The specific areas build on the prime areas and grow in importance as children get older:

- literacy;
- mathematics;
- understanding the world;
- expressive arts and design.

## How children learn in the EYFS

### Learning through play

Children learn through play, with a mix of activities they choose and ones adults guide. Building with blocks becomes early maths, a pretend shop becomes language and social skills, and outdoor play builds strength and balance.

### Three ways of learning

The EYFS also describes how children learn best: by playing and exploring, by being active and keeping at it, and by creating and thinking for themselves. Teachers look for these habits as much as for what children know.

### A key person

Each child has a key person, one adult who knows them well, helps them settle and keeps in touch with parents. For babies and toddlers in particular, this steady relationship makes nursery feel safe.

### How progress is tracked

Teachers observe each child and plan the next steps around them. At the end of the Foundation Stage, children are assessed against the Early Learning Goals, which describe what most children can do by the age of five. This isn't a test: it's based on what teachers have seen over the year.

## A typical day in an EYFS nursery

Every nursery organises its day differently, but an EYFS day often includes:

- a welcome and a short group time;
- free play, both indoors and outdoors;
- small-group activities led by a teacher;
- stories, songs and music;
- meals, snacks and rest for younger children;
- time to talk about the day before home time.

## EYFS vs Montessori

Montessori is a widely used approach in Dubai and around the world. Both approaches respect children as active learners, but they organise the day differently.

### Montessori in brief

Montessori nurseries often mix ages in one class, so younger and older children learn together. Classrooms use special Montessori materials, each designed for a particular skill, and children choose their own work for long stretches. The teacher observes and guides rather than leading the whole group.

### EYFS in brief

EYFS groups are usually by age. Teachers combine free play with guided activities and plan around each child's interests and next steps. Children have more group times, and the seven areas of learning shape what is planned.

### What they share

Both value independence, curiosity and learning by doing. Both expect adults to watch children closely and respond to what they need. They simply organise the day differently.

### Which suits your child?

There's no single right answer. Some children thrive with long periods of self-directed work, others enjoy a mix of free play and group activities. Think about where your child will go to school, too. If a British school is the plan, an EYFS nursery leads directly into the British curriculum.

## Other approaches in Dubai

You may also see Reggio Emilia nurseries, which build learning around children's projects and interests, and American-curriculum nurseries, which lead into American schools. When you visit, ask each nursery how its approach prepares children for the next step.

## From EYFS to FS1 and FS2

In British schools, the EYFS continues into FS1 and FS2. Staying at a nursery that teaches up to FS2 means your child can finish the Foundation Stage in a familiar place before starting school.

From the 2026–27 academic year, a child's age for FS1 and FS2 is counted on 31 December of the year they start. A child needs to be three by 31 December to start FS1 and four to start FS2.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the EYFS the same as the British curriculum?

The EYFS is the early years part of the British curriculum. It covers children from birth to five, up to the end of FS2.

### Can my child move from a Montessori nursery to a British school?

Yes. Children move between approaches all the time. Teachers in FS1, FS2 and Year 1 are used to welcoming children from different nurseries.

### What are the Early Learning Goals?

They describe what most children can do by the end of the Foundation Stage, across the seven areas of learning.
