# How to Choose a British Nursery in Dubai: A Guide for New Families

Moving to Dubai with a young child means finding a nursery quickly, often in a city you're still getting to know. You may not know the areas yet, the terms on nursery websites can be confusing, and there's rarely family nearby to help. Here's what to compare, which questions to ask, and how to make the first weeks easier for your child.

## Nursery, preschool, kindergarten or daycare?

Parents in Dubai use these words in different ways, so it helps to know what each one usually means here.

- **Nursery** is the most common word for an early childhood centre. A licensed nursery in Dubai can take children from 45 days to six years old.
- **Preschool** usually describes the years before school, roughly from two or three to five.
- **Kindergarten** often refers to KG1 and KG2 classes, which are part of many schools.
- **Daycare** is used loosely for childcare, often for babies and toddlers.
- **FS1 and FS2** are the British names for the Foundation Stage years, the first two years of a British school or the oldest groups in a British nursery.

Whatever name a centre uses, check that it holds a KHDA licence. Our guide to KHDA and nurseries explains how.

## Start with the curriculum

Most British nurseries in Dubai follow the EYFS (Early Years Foundation Stage), the framework used in England for children from birth to five. It's play-based, and it leads into FS1 and FS2, the first years of British schooling. If your child will join a British school later, an EYFS nursery keeps the path familiar: the same routines, the same language of learning and the same goals.

Dubai also has Montessori, Reggio Emilia and American-curriculum nurseries. Each has its strengths. Read our guide to the EYFS and Montessori to see how the two most common approaches compare.

## What to compare

### Location and hours

A short drive matters every day. Start by searching for a nursery near me, then check the route at the times you'll actually drive it. Families in Arjan and Al Barsha South often look at nurseries close to home, while others prefer one near work.

Check opening hours too. They can decide whether a nursery works for two working parents, and whether you need extra help at the start or end of the day.

### Space indoors and out

Young children learn by moving, climbing and exploring. Look at the outdoor area, and ask where children play during the hottest months, from May to September. An indoor gym or hall makes a real difference in summer.

### Staff and care

Ask how long teachers have been with the nursery, what training they have and who looks after health and first aid. It's also worth asking whether there is a nurse on site and how the nursery keeps you informed during the day.

### Food and daily routine

Some nurseries provide meals, others ask parents to send lunch. Ask about the daily routine, nap times and how the nursery handles allergies.

### Language and values

Most British nurseries teach in English. Some families also want Arabic or Islamic studies alongside English, so ask how these are included in the day.

### Fees and what's included

Nursery fees in Dubai vary a lot, and so does what they cover. When you compare prices, ask what's included: hours, meals, uniform, trips, holiday camps and any registration fee or deposit.

## Is there a "best nursery in Dubai"?

Many parents start by searching for the best nursery in Dubai. In practice, the best nursery is the one that fits your child and your family: the right curriculum, a reasonable drive, hours that work and a team you trust. Visiting two or three nurseries usually makes the choice clear.

## Starting mid-year and age rules

Families arrive in Dubai all year round, so ask whether the nursery accepts children during the year. For older children, check the age rules. From the 2026–27 academic year, a child's age for FS1 and FS2 is counted on 31 December of the year they start, rather than 31 August. A child needs to be three by 31 December to start FS1 and four to start FS2.

## Settling in after a move

A new country and a new nursery at once is a lot for a small child. A few steps help:

- Ask about a gradual start, with shorter days in the first week.
- Keep mornings predictable, with the same order of things each day.
- Bring a comfort toy or something familiar from home.
- Say goodbye briefly and calmly, and tell your child when you'll be back.
- Give your child a few weeks to adjust before you judge how it's going.

## Questions to ask on a tour

- Is the nursery licensed by KHDA, and what is its permit number?
- Which curriculum do you follow, and how does it lead into school?
- Is there a nurse on site?
- Where do children play in the summer months?
- What does a typical day look like?
- How does settling in work?
- What do the fees include?

## Frequently asked questions

### What age can a child start nursery in Dubai?

Licensed nurseries in Dubai can accept children from 45 days old.

### What is the difference between a British nursery and a school FS1 class?

Both can follow the EYFS. A nursery often has smaller groups and younger children, while a school FS1 class is part of a larger school.
